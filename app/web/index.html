A coverage check that shows its work.

Give it a question, a claim somebody wrote (“the jewelry theft limit is $1,000”), and the documents that might answer it. It says whether the claim is right, which document decided it, which clause, and which page. When the documents cannot settle it, it says so instead of picking one.

Run a worked packet Upload your documents

What it will not do: read document families it has not seen, check coverage at volume, answer a question outside its fixed list, or give a verdict on a wording claim.

Most tools sell a better reader. A better reader still gives you an answer you cannot verify without re-reading. This sells an answer checkable in seconds, and a refusal when the documents cannot support one.

For the person answering

The governing document, the clause and the page, in seconds, with a verdict on the amount or a named reason it cannot decide.

For the person signing off

Every answer carries its own evidence, so the file can be defended without re-reading it.

Three answers, and no guessing

Amount claims get one of three answers. Two documents speaking to the same amount are compared, not merged, and only on the same currency and basis. When a rule settles which one governs, the rule is printed: an endorsement beats the form it amends, a later effective date beats an earlier one.

  • The claim is right. A document governs this line and states the same amount.
  • The claim is wrong. A document governs this line and states a different amount. Both amounts are shown.
  • This can’t be settled from these documents, with one named reason:
    • two documents state different amounts and no rule picks a winner;
    • the amounts cannot be compared, and the mismatch is named;
    • the document that would govern is missing from the packet;
    • no document addresses the question.

A wording claim (“the endorsement covers misplacing or losing jewelry”) gets the governing clause quoted with its page, marked quoted, not checked. Nothing is guessed.

Every answer can be checked

Each answer rests on assertions. Each assertion carries its document, its place in it (a page, or character offsets in a text filing) and the exact quote, and a strength for every step: read straight off the page, derived by code from what was read, or judgment, from the model’s reading or a person’s identification. The weakest step is always named, so you know which part to check first.

What it refuses

Every refusal is a result with its reason, styled like a verdict, because it is one.

  • A document with no readable text layer.
  • A document it cannot identify, which the person cannot place either.
  • A question that is not on its list.
  • A packet where an endorsement amends a document that is not in the packet.

Questions

Is this another chatbot?

No. It checks one claim, and every value it reports is a quote checked against the file. The model reads and points; code compares numbers and applies precedence.

What if an answer is wrong?

Every value is boxed on its page so you can check it in seconds. A quote that cannot be found exactly on its page is rejected and never decides an answer.

Which documents work?

Two document families so far: a 1996 D&O filing from SEC EDGAR, and ISO homeowners forms (PDF). You can upload your own PDFs: the system proposes what each document is from what is printed on it, and nothing runs until you confirm or correct every row. It answers only questions on its fixed list.

Is this a coverage opinion?

No. It compares the amounts the documents state with the amount in the claim, and shows which document controls by a stated rule. It is not a coverage determination and not legal advice.
